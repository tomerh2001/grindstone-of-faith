An exact probability model

Let M be the maximum stones, n the amount per attempt, s the stone price and f its polishing fee. The success rate is p = n/M and each attempt costs C = n(s + f).

E[total] = C/p = M(s + f)
SD[total] = M(s + f) × √(1 − p)
P(total ≤ B) = 1 − (1 − p)⌊B/C⌋

For p < 1, the q-quantile is C × ⌈ln(1 − q) / ln(1 − p)⌉. For p = 1, all outcomes cost C. For q = 1 and p < 1, no finite retry budget guarantees success.

What “most cost effective” means

We minimize average spending to finish one eligible upgrade. Every legal batch ties. The calculator chooses the one with the smallest cost variance: the full batch.

A smaller batch can have a lower median or even a lower P95. For example, 19 Faith succeeds 95% of the time on the first attempt. That does not make its average cost lower; the remaining 5% must fund retries.

At a constant unit price, even changing the batch after failures cannot lower the average of a policy that continues until success. Buying at a lower price reduces the bill; choosing smaller batches does not.

Assumptions and limits

Retry assumptions: attempts are independent, all stones and fees are consumed on failure, the ring stays eligible at the same level, and there is no pity. The reviewed patch notes do not explicitly document every failure detail. The full-batch guarantee follows directly from the published rates.

Prices stay fixed, stones are acquired as needed, and retries continue until success. The ring’s purchase cost, resale fees, market changes, borrowing costs, and the value of waiting are excluded. Costs cover Life or Faith separately, not a combined level 4 → 6 upgrade. A zero purchase price still incurs polishing fees.

Stopping without an upgrade lowers measured spending but changes the objective. Finite screenshot supply does not fund an unlimited retry tail at the same price. All supplied screenshots concern Faith; no Life market price was supplied.

Simulations use seeded random trials to illustrate the mathematical model; they do not measure the game’s random number generator. Probabilities and the recommended policy are exact model results, independent of sampling noise.
