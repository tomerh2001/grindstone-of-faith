GMS v271 mechanics

Faith accepts up to 20 stones together, adding five percentage points and a 1b fee per stone. Ring of Restraint and Continuous Ring remain eligible. Read Nexon’s current rules.

EXACT MODEL

The math is simpler than Star Force.

For n stones per attempt and price s billion mesos per stone:

p = n / 20
C = n × (s + 1)
E[total cost] = C / p = 20 × (s + 1)

With independent attempts and no pity, the attempt count is geometric. After k attempts, success chance is 1 − (1 − p)k. To reach a target q, take the ceiling of ln(1 − q) / ln(1 − p) attempts.

Standard deviation of total cost is 20 × (s + 1) × √(1 − p). At 20 stones it becomes zero. At any smaller batch, the unlucky tail is unbounded.

For p = 1, success takes exactly one attempt. For q = 1 and p < 1, no finite attempt count suffices.

DECISION RULE

Choose a chance, then fund that attempt.

The fewest stones for a single-attempt success chance of at least q is ⌈20q⌉. A 95% target needs 19; a 99% target rounds up to 20.

For a maximum spend of 20 stones or fewer, splitting the same allowance across attempts cannot beat using it together. This follows from the union bound: the total success chance is at most the sum of the individual attempt probabilities.

Across completed upgrades, every strategy that continues until success has the same expected 20 stones and 20b fees at a constant price—even if you change batch sizes after a failure.

Smaller attempts buy a chance to finish cheaply. They do not lower the average cost of finishing.

Assumptions, limits, and what “optimal” means

The main answer minimizes expected total spending to finish one eligible level-5 ring, at a fixed nonnegative stone price with a linear polishing fee. All batch sizes tie; 20 is the practical recommendation because it also removes variance. The optional one-attempt target instead minimizes the upfront commitment for your selected probability.

Retry calculations assume independent outcomes, no pity or failure bonus, and purchases only when the next attempt is needed. They exclude the cost of obtaining the level-5 ring, trading fees on resale, future market changes, borrowing costs, and the value of waiting. Already-owned tradable stones still have an opportunity cost.

Order-book totals use only the visible supply. Long retry tails at a flat price are hypothetical: they do not imply enough stones are currently available at that price. An unfinished attempt sequence can look cheap because some players never get the upgrade.

Price sample summaries are descriptive only. We do not bootstrap confidence intervals from sorted, incomplete screenshot pages or infer game success rates from market data. Simulation validates the modeled distribution; it is not evidence that Nexon’s random number generator follows it.
